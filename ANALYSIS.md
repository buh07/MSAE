## Current synthesis after August follow-ups and source-gate disposition (2026-09-15)

**Current decision: keep K2 and branch training closed.** The project now has a
stronger positive-control and capacity story, but no evidence that rescues the
original semantic-branch interpretation. The exposed AMALGUM v3 source cannot
serve as independent replication, and its gen9 control plane was rejected before
any model scoring. A genuinely new source must pass source-family, support,
official-role, and full accessible-history overlap gates before any scoring; even
a successful source gate would authorize only a future prescore protocol, not
training.

The most durable conclusions are:

1. **Encoding is not separability.** Positional, structural, lexical, and
   contextual signals are decodable, and learned K2 geometry is highly stable,
   but the eligible v2.4, relational, and context/local studies do not show
   private functional ownership. Relative-quartile leakage exceeded assigned
   recovery in the headline g4 comparison, relation-aware features did not beat
   matched alternatives consistently, and coherent context barely exceeded
   unrelated context.
2. **Potency is not precision.** Proxy benchmarks repeatedly find methods that
   move the target behavior, but increased recovery often accompanies leakage or
   collateral damage. The v2 joint gate passed for none of 972 controlled method
   summaries. The released-method v3 natural endpoint was itself ineligible, so
   it cannot support an all-method negative.
3. **Contribution is not sufficient control.** IOI and induction assays showed
   real directional behavior and causal contribution while failing the full
   recovery/specificity qualification. Exact full-residual and constructed
   controls demonstrate that the evaluator can return a positive result when a
   complete mechanism is supplied.
4. **Capacity matters, but basis span is not the whole explanation.** In trained
   synthetic copies, paired/global and several donor-compression controllers pass
   once rank is adequate, with the supported sufficient-rank transition bracketed
   in `(16,32]`. SAE-capacity R2 is the primary SAE result: full-code patching
   passed while every registered sub-full subset failed despite low reconstruction
   error. Since budget-64 decoder spans already cover the successful PCA span,
   missing span alone cannot explain the subset failures.
5. **The August successor is a generalization warning, not a basis indictment.**
   Its 64-row fit changed data quantity, seeds, and panels relative to R2. Excellent
   fit error degraded to roughly 0.6 held-out relative error, while every selected
   span was empirically full rank. The failure therefore does not isolate basis
   learning, feature selection, coefficients, or conditioning. R2 remains primary.
6. **Bridge v2 repaired machinery but produced no bridge-method result.** All 492
   supported endpoints passed its exact control checks, but 20 endpoints missed
   the support floor and the protocol stopped before validation. Calling this a
   method comparison would cross the evidence boundary.
7. **Gen9 is closed as an implementation rejection.** Safe predecessor and CPU
   tests exposed stale authority hashes, failed changed-region construction, a
   misbound gen7 review, incomplete static closure, containment-node mismatch, a
   stale socket namespace, and missing publication/recovery definitions. Because
   the frozen sequence stops on those failures, omitting changed-region
   publication, real containment, capability probing, M3/M4, signing, and launch
   is the correct completion of that candidate—not unfinished execution.
8. **V10 did not yield a blind payload.** It acquired and cryptographically bound
   the configured UniversalDependencies Swedish-Talbanken repository tree. Its
   source-family and candidate-pedigree screens were eligible,
   but the frozen cross-role gate retained 100 fail-fast witnesses (98 short
   containments and two exact overlaps). The protocol therefore stopped before
   full history-overlap/support evaluation, split publication, or private payload
   creation. This identifies source/role design as the next observed blocker; it
   does not exclude later history-overlap or support blockers, is not a model
   result, and does not establish independent replication, researcher unawareness,
   or model-pretraining independence.

### Evidence hierarchy and next legitimate step

The scientific hierarchy is now: exact/constructed controls establish evaluator
reachability; trained-copy studies establish conditional capacity results; natural
and pretrained-model assays test external validity; only endpoints clearing their
own support and specificity gates can adjudicate representation methods. Positive
metrics below a failed common gate remain descriptive. Technical eligibility is
not scientific success, and a technical stop is not a negative representation
result.

The only legitimate immediate continuation is a **new reviewed source protocol**:
prospectively freeze either a genuinely new source or an outcome-blind role/group
construction that prevents cross-role leakage, then establish license,
source-family, pedigree, cross-role separation, full accessible-history overlap,
and support before publishing a replacement payload. V10 itself is terminal and
must not be repaired or retried. Do not reopen AMALGUM as confirmation, do not
lower old gates, and do not restart K2 or train a new branch before a separately
reviewed independent scoring protocol exists.

## Executive Summary

The project has cleared the main conceptual gate for MSAE and has also cleared the basic engineering gate for training a K=2 model at scale.

The strongest supported conclusions are:
- The raw-activation premise is supported for the target setting. In the final pre-K2 suite, Pythia-160M at layers 3 and 4 passes the v2 separability gate at ranks 8 and 16 across IID, source-holdout, and corpus-holdout conditions, with a clean failure boundary at rank 32. The authoritative evidence is the final v6 full suite and the packaged pre-K2 reports in [reports/pre_k2_suite_v6](reports/pre_k2_suite_v6).
- Layer 3 is the best primary site for K=2 training. Layer 4 remains a legitimate fallback, but layer 3 consistently has the stronger position signal and the cleaner overall recommendation in the final pre-K2 decision artifact: [pre_k2_decision.md](reports/pre_k2_suite_v6/pre_k2_decision.md).
- The custom K=2 trainer is real, stable, resumable, and can be run at 1B-token scale with GPU-first execution. The smoke tests, resume tests, wave-1, and the completed fastdata-stream wave-2 run all support this.
- The K=2 architecture also looks operationally healthy in terms of latent usage. In the completed fastdata-stream runs, active-latent fractions remain high in both branches while the revival path appears to be supporting training rather than merely rescuing widespread collapse.
- The incoherence regularizer is doing meaningful work. In the completed fastdata-stream wave-2 run, regularized layer-3 models consistently reach much lower incoherence than the no-regularizer control. The matched-seed comparison is the cleanest evidence for that claim.

The most important nuance is that the current evidence supports a stronger claim about **branch separation** than about **reconstruction improvement**. In the completed 1B-token wave, the regularized runs are clearly better on incoherence, but they also show a modest and fairly consistent reconstruction cost relative to the no-regularizer control. That means the current quadratic cross-branch penalty is validated as useful, but not yet validated as the final or optimal coherence penalty.

## How to Read the Evidence

The experiment history falls into three broad stages:
1. Early raw-activation pilots that established feasibility and exposed metric-calibration problems.
2. A final pre-K2 separability suite that should be treated as the authoritative gate for whether K=2 training was justified.
3. K=2 training runs that test whether the two-branch architecture and incoherence regularizer behave as expected under real optimization.

The final pre-K2 suite is the best source for answering “should this project proceed to K=2 training?”
The completed fastdata-stream wave-2 run is the best source for answering “what does K=2 training actually look like at scale?”

Older runs remain useful because they show how the criteria and infrastructure evolved, but several of them were explicitly superseded.

## Phase 1: What the Raw-Activation Pilots Established

### The early runs were mostly about de-risking and metric development

The first raw-activation pilot runs in [20260526_201536](pilot_runs/20260526_201536) and [20260526_224002_saga3000_c2alt](pilot_runs/20260526_224002_saga3000_c2alt) are best understood as infrastructure and criterion-shaping runs, not final scientific verdicts.

They established several things that mattered later:
- the basic probe-based decomposition could be run on small and medium models,
- the project needed GPU-first probe training rather than CPU-heavy sklearn-only fitting,
- and the original criterion set was too brittle to serve as the final gate.

That is why the early runs show many `all_pass=0` outcomes in [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md) without invalidating the project. The later runs demonstrate that those zeros mostly reflected criterion design and optimization issues rather than a failure of the central hypothesis.

### The torch/GPU probe migration was a turning point

The move to [20260527_124434_torch_gpu_probes](pilot_runs/20260527_124434_torch_gpu_probes) mattered for two reasons:
- it removed the main runtime bottleneck in the probe stage,
- and it made it possible to run richer rank and split sweeps without the experiment being dominated by CPU optimizer behavior.

That run still had `all_pass=0/4`, but it also showed that `c4=4/4`, which means the principal-angle orthogonality signal was already present even before the final metric policy was stabilized. In retrospect, that run looks like an early sign that the geometry was promising while the gating logic was still too strict or miscalibrated.

### The rank-curve and v5 multiseed runs changed the question from “is there signal?” to “what is the right gate?”

The balanced rank-curve run [20260527_140327_rankcurve_c2var_balanced](pilot_runs/20260527_140327_rankcurve_c2var_balanced) is important because it showed:
- `c1_new_rank_recovery=34/34`,
- `c4=34/34`,
- but no full passes under the then-current combined gates.

That combination matters. It indicates that low-rank recoverability and near-orthogonality were robust, but the combined gate logic was still not aligned with the actual structure in the data.

The v5 multiseed run [20260527_210247_v5_gpufirst_balanced_multiseed](pilot_runs/20260527_210247_v5_gpufirst_balanced_multiseed) is where the project starts to look scientifically decisive rather than exploratory:
- `all_pass_v2=22/54`,
- all three Pythia-160M seeds pass at layer 3, ranks 8 and 16,
- and the pass pattern becomes structured rather than noisy.

That structured pass pattern is the key point. It is not random or weakly scattered. Instead, it is concentrated in exactly the model/layer/rank regime that the project would later choose for K=2 training.

## Phase 2: What the Final Pre-K2 Suite Established

### The authoritative pre-K2 result is the final v6 full suite

The final pre-K2 suite is [20260528_001543_v6_prek2_fullsuite](pilot_runs/20260528_001543_v6_prek2_fullsuite), with its packaged report bundle in [reports/pre_k2_suite_v6](reports/pre_k2_suite_v6).

This is the run that should be treated as the project’s final answer to the question:
“Do we have enough evidence that a K=2 position/content decomposition is plausible to justify real MSAE training?”

The answer from this suite is yes.

The strongest evidence is the Pythia-160M headline result recorded in [reports/pre_k2_suite_v6/RESULTS.md](reports/pre_k2_suite_v6/RESULTS.md):
- `92/92` passes for `r8/r16` across all split modes,
- `0/46` passes at the boundary diagnostic rank `32`.

That is an unusually clean result. It tells us several things at once:
- the success regime is not marginal,
- the pass/fail boundary is sharp rather than diffuse,
- and the result generalizes across IID, source-holdout, and corpus-holdout settings.

### Why this matters scientifically

This outcome is exactly what a useful pre-K2 gate should look like.

If the underlying geometry were weak, unstable, or dataset-specific, we would expect one or more of the following:
- substantial seed-level disagreement,
- a collapse under holdout settings,
- or a smeared rank boundary where `r32` still passes often.

Instead, the final suite shows:
- perfect headline pass rates for both `L3` and `L4`,
- perfect holdout-headline pass rates,
- and total failure at the boundary rank.

That means the pilot did not just find a way to pass its own gate on a narrow in-sample setup. It found a regime that looks structurally real and reproducible.

### Layer 3 versus layer 4

The final decision artifact [pre_k2_decision.md](reports/pre_k2_suite_v6/pre_k2_decision.md) recommends:
- primary layer: `L3`
- fallback layer: `L4`

This is the right level of confidence.

The data supports saying that `L3` is better, but not that `L4` is invalid. Both pass the headline gate everywhere it matters. The difference is one of preference and margin, not of success versus failure.

That distinction is important operationally. It justified starting K=2 training on `L3`, while keeping `L4` available as a hedge instead of discarding it entirely.

### The compatibility sweep changed how absolute AUC numbers should be interpreted

The compatibility report [compatibility_iid_v5_vs_v6.md](reports/pre_k2_suite_v6/compatibility_iid_v5_vs_v6.md) is easy to underrate, but it is actually one of the most important methodological outputs in the project.

It shows that the drop in raw position AUC from earlier runs to the final v6 summary was not just seed noise. A real part of that shift came from the data regime itself:
- `L3` data-regime effect: about `-0.0215`
- `L4` data-regime effect: about `-0.0301`

That means absolute AUC values cannot be compared casually across experimental regimes. The final project story should therefore lean more heavily on:
- pass-rate stability,
- split robustness,
- and within-regime comparisons,

than on any single raw AUC headline number taken out of context.

This is a strength, not a weakness. It means the project already discovered and documented one of the main sources of reporting fragility before paper drafting.

Wave 1 was therefore a genuine green light for scaling, not just a smoke test.

- the regularized L3 runs had much lower incoherence than the no-inc control,
- while reconstruction stayed in a similar band.

At this stage, the project’s theory and practice were still aligned in the most optimistic way.

## Phase 4: What the Superseded Long Wave-2 Run Means

The initial long wave-2 run [20260529_122259_k2_msae_wave2](pilot_runs/20260529_122259_k2_msae_wave2) should not be treated as the final scientific result.

Its status snapshot shows very poor FVU and triggered a regression alert in [wave2_status.md](pilot_runs/20260529_122259_k2_msae_wave2/reports/wave2_status.md). In context, this run is best interpreted as an infrastructure and data-pipeline failure mode rather than a fair test of the model design.

That distinction matters a lot. If we mixed this run uncritically into the project conclusion, we would underestimate the model and overestimate the architectural risk. The later fastdata-stream rerun corrected the main data-loading problem and produced materially different results. That is why [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md) marks this run as superseded.

So the right lesson from this run is not “K=2 MSAE fails at scale.”
The right lesson is “the original long-run data pipeline was not suitable for a clean 1B-token training evaluation.”

## Phase 5: What the Fastdata-Stream Wave-2 Run Shows

The authoritative completed training run is [20260601_132158_k2_msae_wave2_fastdata_stream](pilot_runs/20260601_132158_k2_msae_wave2_fastdata_stream).

This run matters because it fixes the earlier throughput pathology and therefore gives us the cleanest large-scale K=2 evidence so far.

### Operationally, the infrastructure fix worked

The fastdata-stream run shows that the data-loader changes were not cosmetic. They changed the experiment from a compromised long run into a usable one.

That means the project now has:
- a stable GPU-first training path,
- a viable resume workflow,
- and a large-scale run whose metrics should be taken seriously.

This is an important project milestone in its own right.

### Utilization health is better than a typical SAE regime

One engineering result that deserves to be called out explicitly is latent utilization. In the completed fastdata-stream runs recorded in [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md), the active-latent fractions remain high in both branches:
- `g4`: position `0.887`, content `0.911`
- `g5`: position `0.897`, content `0.926`
- `g6`: position `0.896`, content `0.935`
- `g7`: position `0.923`, content `0.930`

That does not prove that MSAE has solved the dead-feature problem in general, and it should not be written up that way. But it does show that the current branch design is operating in a healthy regime rather than a regime where most atoms are inactive and the revival path is constantly triaging collapse.

This matters because standard SAE training often needs substantial auxiliary engineering around dead features and resampling. The current K=2 trainer looks better than that baseline on branch utilization alone, which is a meaningful engineering result even before we ask whether the decomposition is the right scientific one.

### Scientifically, the incoherence regularizer is clearly effective

The most important causal comparison is the matched-seed pair:
- `g4`: layer 3, seed 42, `lambda_inc=1e-2`
- `g7`: layer 3, seed 42, `lambda_inc=0`

From the completed summaries in [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md):
- `g4` tail-100 FVU: `0.076385`
- `g4` tail-100 incoherence: `0.004643`
- `g7` tail-100 FVU: `0.069778`
- `g7` tail-100 incoherence: `0.015200`

This is the cleanest current statement of the regularizer effect:
- incoherence is reduced by about `3.3x`,
- but reconstruction is modestly worse in the regularized matched-seed run.

That is still a meaningful success for the regularizer. It shows that the coherence term is not merely decorative; it really changes the learned geometry. But it also means that the current penalty is not a pure free lunch.

### The regularized regime remains viable across seeds

The second regularized seed, `g5`, is especially informative:
- tail-100 FVU: `0.071359`
- tail-100 incoherence: `0.003150`

This run is very close to the no-inc control on reconstruction while remaining far better on incoherence.

That matters because it keeps the overall story from collapsing into a simple story of a large reconstruction penalty. The more accurate statement is:
- the regularized regime consistently buys much lower incoherence,
- and the reconstruction cost is modest, though not zero.

That is a richer and more realistic result.

### The third regularized seed recovered by completion

By the end of the run recorded in [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md), `g6` finished with:
- tail-100 FVU: `0.073543`
- tail-100 incoherence: `0.002811`

That final result matters because it is materially better than the earlier live snapshots suggested. Mid-run, `g6` looked like a possible underperforming regularized seed. By completion, it no longer looks like an alarming outlier:
- its reconstruction lands between `g4` and `g5`,
- and its incoherence is the lowest of the completed runs.

So `g6` now reads as:
- another strong confirmation that the regularizer can drive incoherence very low,
- with some real but not dramatic seed-level variation in reconstruction quality.

This still leaves room for optimization-path questions. Plausible explanations for the remaining spread include:
- data-order effects,
- dictionary initialization effects,
- branch competition or early atom-claim dynamics,
- and optimizer path dependence interacting with the branch sparsity budgets.

### The regularized regime remains viable across seeds, but reconstruction quality is not uniform

The completed wave therefore supports a robust claim about branch separation, but it still does not support a claim that all regularized seeds land in exactly the same reconstruction regime.

It also now supports a cleaner statement than the mid-run snapshots did: in this completed wave, all three regularized seeds finish with lower incoherence and slightly higher FVU than the no-inc control. So the completed evidence points to a consistent, modest reconstruction cost rather than a mixed sign on reconstruction.

That is not a reason to doubt the whole setup. The spread is much narrower than the earlier live read suggested, but it is still enough to justify studying optimization-path variance before making overly clean reconstruction claims about the regularized regime as a whole.

## What the Results Mean for the Project as a Whole

### 1. The project is past the “is this premise even real?” stage

The pre-K2 suite answers that clearly. The raw-activation position/content decomposition is not an illusion created by one probe, one split, or one seed. It survives:
- multiseed testing,
- source holdout,
- corpus holdout,
- and a sharp rank boundary test.

That means the project’s central motivating hypothesis is no longer speculative. It is empirically grounded enough to justify real training work.

### 2. The project has identified a defensible default training regime

The strongest current default is:
- model: `Pythia-160M`
- layer: `3`
- branch count: `K=2`
- regularized baseline: `lambda_inc=1e-2`

That configuration is not arbitrary anymore. It is the output of a staged experimental process that:
- chose the layer using split-robust pilot evidence,
- chose the rank window using a clean pass/fail boundary,
- and chose the regularizer by direct ablation against a no-inc control.

### 3. The project’s biggest remaining modeling question is now about the coherence penalty itself

Earlier in the project, the hard question was whether the decomposition existed at all.
Now the hard question is narrower and more interesting:

How should coherence regularization be shaped so that the model preserves the separation benefit while minimizing reconstruction cost?

That question exists precisely because the current results are already strong enough to move beyond premise validation. The next step is probably not a large expensive `lambda` grid, but it is also too early to declare the current working point fully optimized.

### 4. The project is in a better position than it would be if the current result were simply “everything passes”

A perfectly one-sided result would be easier to write up, but less informative scientifically.
What we have instead is more realistic and more useful:
- a strong, reproducible separation effect,
- a viable large-scale training setup,
- and a measurable reconstruction/separation tradeoff.

That is exactly the kind of result that can motivate the next experiment cleanly.

## Main Open Questions After These Results

The results narrow the open questions considerably.

### 1. Is `lambda_inc=1e-2` the right regularization strength, or just a good working point?

The matched-seed comparison shows that `lambda_inc=1e-2` clearly improves incoherence, and the completed regularized runs suggest that this is already a useful and stable operating regime. What the current evidence does not show is that this value is optimal for the tradeoff between:
- branch separation,
- reconstruction,
- and latent utilization quality.

So the current evidence suggests that a large `lambda` sweep is not the highest-priority next step, but it does not justify treating `0.01` as solved. A proportionate next check would be something like:
- `lambda_inc in {0.001, 0.01}`
- and optionally `0.03` later if over-regularization specifically becomes the question

### 2. How does K=2 MSAE compare to a standard single-branch SAE at comparable sparsity and scale?

The current absolute FVU values are meaningful internally, but they are difficult to interpret fully without a standard-SAE comparator at roughly comparable sparsity, token budget, and model/layer site.

That omission is understandable in a project-state document because the baseline has not yet been run. But for paper-facing claims it is not optional. The project needs to know whether the K=2 architecture is:
- paying a meaningful reconstruction tax for branch structure,
- roughly competitive with a standard SAE,
- or unexpectedly better than the most relevant single-branch baseline.

This should be treated as a must-run comparator before publication-quality comparative claims are made.

### 3. Is the current quadratic cross-branch penalty the best coherence penalty?

The present training results make this the most natural next question.
The project now has enough evidence to justify moving from:
- “should there be a coherence term at all?”

to:
- “what penalty family and penalty scope best supports the project’s objectives?”

### 4. How much of the seed variance in reconstruction is fundamental versus optimizable?

The completed fastdata-stream run suggests that the regularized regime is viable, but not perfectly uniform in reconstruction quality across seeds. The final `g4/g5/g6` spread is smaller than the earlier live snapshots suggested, but it is still real. The remaining gap could come from:
- optimization trajectory,
- data-order effects,
- dictionary initialization effects,
- branch competition dynamics,
- or regularization geometry.

This is now a sharper question than whether the project works in principle.

## Recommended Interpretation for Writing and Planning

If this project were being summarized for a methods section or internal milestone report today, the most defensible framing would be:

- The raw-activation premise is supported and robust under multiseed and holdout testing.
- Layer 3 on Pythia-160M is the best supported primary training site; layer 4 remains a fallback.
- The K=2 trainer is stable and scalable after the fastdata-stream pipeline fix.
- The K=2 trainer also appears to be operating in a healthy latent-utilization regime rather than a dead-feature-heavy one.
- The incoherence regularizer materially improves branch separation; the strongest claim is about geometry and specialization, not reconstruction gains.
- In this completed wave, reconstruction under regularization is consistently slightly worse than the no-inc control, but the cost is modest and the regularized seeds remain in a tight usable band.
- The next model-side priorities should be: a small `lambda` calibration check, a standard single-branch SAE comparator, and then broader coherence-family ablations if those still look necessary.

That framing is strong, accurate, and aligned with the actual evidence trail in [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md).
